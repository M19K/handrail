04

The arrow

The only thing Handrail draws outside its own window, and the thing people will see in a screenshot of it. Every value below is already proven working on a 2× display with a negative origin.

Razor

Select the Razor tool

Step 2 of 5
14px tip gap — never touches the control
2.6px mint over a 7px black casing
  • Never draw the mint stroke without the dark casing. Mint on white is about 1.9:1 — illegible. The casing means the marker carries its own contrast onto any background instead of assuming one.
  • The tip stops 14px short of the control. The user has to be able to click the thing being pointed at; the overlay is click-through, but a tip sitting on the target reads as covering it.
  • The arrow approaches from whichever side has the most room. Otherwise the tail runs off-screen, or the label lands on top of what it's describing.
  • One arrow at a time. Two arrows is a diagram, not an instruction.
  • The pulse is the only ambient motion in the product and it stops entirely under reduced-motion.

Decisions this makes, and one it doesn't

Decided here: onboarding is a one-time real window, not an overlay panel — it asks for a secret and makes a privacy promise, and neither belongs in a 30px strip. That closes an open question in PRODUCT.md.

Decided here: the capture indicator is a transient chip in the bar, not a persistent badge. It confirms something happened, then stops taking up room.

Still open: how Handrail knows a step is finished. The mockups show step 2 as current, but nothing here decides whether the user ticks it, or Handrail watches the screen and works it out. That's a product question with a real cost attached, and it's the next one worth arguing about.
