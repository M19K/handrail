Four marks

Handrail's logo has an unusually hard job: it has to work at 16 pixels in a system tray and inside a 30-pixel pill, on top of software it doesn't control. Anything with more than two elements dies at that size. All four below are drawn on the same 24-unit grid with the same stroke weight.

Must survive16px in the tray, and inside the collapsed pill
Must workOn dark and light, in mint and in flat white
Must notLook like a menu icon, a table, or a wifi symbol
ShouldSay something true about the product, not just look nice
Mark A

Progress Rail

A rail with your position on it. The travelled part is solid, the rest is dim, and the dot sits at the boundary. It is the product described in one shape — a path, and where you are along it — and the dot can slide as steps complete.

Mark B

The Turn

A single continuous stroke: flat, then rising. The shape of an actual stair handrail, and of getting somewhere. One element, so it never breaks at any size.

Mark C

The Rail & Posts

The most literal reading: a horizontal rail carried on two posts. Architectural, steady, a bit institutional in a good way — this is the mark that says "something solid to hold onto" rather than "software".

Rail & Point

The rail, with the product's own arrow resting on it. This is the only mark that contains the feature Handrail is actually built around — the thing that points at the button on your screen.

My recommendation

Mark A, the Progress Rail. It is the only one that describes what the product actually does rather than what it is named after — a path, and your position on it. It also holds up best at 16px, which is where this mark will spend most of its life.

It has a second life as UI. The same shape is a progress indicator: the dot slides right as steps complete. A logo that doubles as a working component is rare and worth taking.

If you want the safest option, take B. One stroke, impossible to break, reads as a stair rail. It is less interesting and it will never be a problem.

C is the most literal and the weakest at small sizes. D is a good idea that fights an existing icon convention — an arrow above a line is already "upload" in most people's heads.
