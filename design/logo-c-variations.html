Handrail · Identity · Mark C, revisited

Four ways to fix C

C was right about one thing and wrong about another. Keeping the part that worked — an unmistakable, steady handrail — and fixing the part that didn't.

What C got right. It is the only mark nobody has to have explained. A rail carried on posts reads as something solid to hold onto, which is the whole promise of the product.

What broke it. Three strokes of equal weight, evenly spaced, on a symmetrical grid. At 16px the eye stops seeing a rail and starts seeing a table — or the Greek letter pi. Symmetry is the bug, not the stroke count.

The fix in every variation below is to break the symmetry — by tilting the rail, by removing a post, by adding a point of interest, or by making it one continuous line.

At 16 pixels, which is where it lives

System tray, taskbar, the collapsed pill. If a mark fails here it fails, whatever it looks like blown up. Original C first, for comparison.

C1

Stair Rail

Tilt the rail. The moment it stops being horizontal it stops being a table, and becomes the thing everybody has actually held — the rail going up a staircase. Posts stay vertical and land on a common floor, which is what sells the slope.

C2

Terminal

Delete a post. One post, off centre, with the rail overhanging it — the end of a handrail rather than a section of one. Two strokes, asymmetric, and it cannot be read as a table because tables don't have one leg.

C3

The Grip

Keep all three strokes but stop them competing. Rail and posts drop back to neutral, and the mint moves to a single grip on the rail — a hand on it, and your position along it. The structure stays literal; the meaning gets added on top. This is C with A's idea grafted in.

C4

One Line

Draw the whole thing without lifting the pen. The rail runs flat, then turns down into its own post — a real handrail terminal, the bit you actually grab at the bottom of a staircase. One element, so there is nothing to break.

My read

C4 with the grip. One continuous stroke gives you C's literal handrail with none of its fragility, and the curve at the end is genuinely the most rail-like shape on this page — it looks like something you would put your hand on. Add the mint grip and it also carries the progress idea, without the four-element clutter of C3.

C2 is the safe pick. Two orthogonal strokes, crisp at every size, impossible to misread. It is also the least memorable.

C1 is the most charming and the most fragile. The tilt solves the table problem beautifully at 32px and up, but diagonals are exactly what goes soft at 16px, which is where this mark lives.

C3 proves a point rather than winning. It shows the grip idea works on a literal rail — but C4 gets the same idea for a quarter of the ink.
